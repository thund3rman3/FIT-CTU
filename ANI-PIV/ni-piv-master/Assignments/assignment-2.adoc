### Objective
Implementation, training, evaluation, and tuning of a *U-Net segmentation model* on the microscopy tubule dataset prepared in xref:../Assignments/assignment-1.adoc[Assignment 1].

---

### Part 1: Dataset Preparation

- Use your dataset from xref:../Assignments/assignment-1.adoc[Assignment 1] (image-mask pairs).
- Split the data into training and validation subsets (optionally consider test set, but remember that our dataset is small).
- Implement a PyTorch `Dataset` and `DataLoader` with appropriate preprocessing (grayscale, fixed size, normalization, binary masks, augmentations, ...).

*Deliverables:* `CiliaDataset` class implementation (or other custom image-mask pair sampling).

---

### Part 2: U-Net Architecture

- Build a custom *U-Net* model in PyTorch.
- Include encoder (downsampling), decoder (upsampling), and skip connections.

*Deliverables:* U-Net model architecture, explanation of the design choices

---

### Part 3: Model Training

- Experiment with various loss functions (`BCE`, Dice loss, or their combinations, ...)
- Track traininig and validation losses

*Deliverables:* training loop implementation, visualizations of the process (training vs. validation loss, Dice score)

---

### Part 4: Model Evaluation 

*Qualitative:* 

- show example predictions vs. ground truth
- identify typical *failure cases* (false positives, missed tubules, noisy masks, ...) + try to explain why this happens

*Quantitative:*

- compute and report accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, dice coefficient (+ FP, FN, TP, TN)
- provide table of quantitative metrics, visualize confusion matrix

---

### Part 5: Hyperparameter Tuning 

- experiment with *U-Net depth* (try small vs. high number of levels)
- experiment with the number of convolutional layers, kernel sizes, pooling types (max/average), ...
- What happens if the network is too shallow or too deep? Which depth worked best and why?
